# Linda (44, middle school social studies teacher, Yonkers) - exit interview

## 1. Arriving
A colleague emailed the link: "could this work for your NYC unit?" I landed on a bright landing page ("Waddle on over to New York.") and thought: Club Penguin, but New York. A kids' game. My first question was whether it is safe for 13 and 14 year olds, so I declined analytics cookies and went straight to the Safety section.

## 2. What I did
1. Declined analytics cookies. Read Safety (ages 13+, chat filtered, "No filter is perfect", Discord and Instagram links) and the FAQ headings.
2. Clicked Play Now. No account needed. Picked Surprise Me for my character, typed a name, ticked "I'm 13 or older".
3. MetroCard photo booth, stamping the card, then the turnstile swipe (failed twice), then Skip Intro.
4. Times Square: Rosa the super offered a tour, I declined. Opened the subway map, rode to Brooklyn Bridge, reached Level 2.
5. On the bridge I clicked the painter's stall (an art shop) and opened the newspaper. Then left.

## 3. Leaving
Around action 33. I had what I needed to answer my colleague. It is charming, but kids would be in an open room with strangers, and I found little actual history in the few minutes I spent. I would not put a class on it as is.

## 4. Fun
- The MetroCard photo booth and stamping. It is a real NYC rite of passage and a kid would love it.
- Times Square and the Brooklyn Bridge art look gorgeous and are friendly for kids.
- The newspaper. "NYC Moments: The Subway Opens" is a real history hook, and the Chinatown soup dumpling story is funny.
- "Please swipe again at this turnstile." Every New Yorker knows it.

## 5. Static
- The painter's stall at the bridge is just a shop for apartment decor. I hoped for the story of the bridge (Roebling, 1883).
- The subway ride is a title card plus a station name. I hoped for a fact about the line.
- The landing page "city so far" board said nobody was on it yet. Weak proof that it is a living place.

## 6. Wanted
- A teacher or class mode: private room, no stranger chat, or chat off.
- Facts and history I could point to, such as a plaque or "learn more" on landmarks, and a way to see what a student learned.
- A way to turn off or hide the "Use a Photo" selfie option for students.
- Something printable or shareable for a lesson plan.

## 7. New York
True: Brooklyn Bridge-City Hall transfers to the 5, 6, J and Z. The turnstile swipe, the bridge promenade with its locks, the pretzel cart, Joe's Shanghai soup dumplings and "folded, never forked" all ring true.
False: I rode the 4 train from Times Square. The 4 does not stop at Times Square (1/2/3/7/N/Q/R/W/S do). My students would catch that. Also I could not verify the dates and facts in the newspaper in this short a visit.

## 8. Coming back
Maybe. Not for myself. I only play Wordle. I would come back if there were a classroom option. I would send it to my colleague with: "Cute and well made, but kids chat with strangers and there's little history. Not for my class unless there's a teacher mode."

## 9. One change
A classroom or teacher mode (invite code, chat off or restricted to preset phrases, a list of landmarks visited, and facts a student can read).

## 10. Bugs and confusion
- The turnstile swipe was hard: failed twice even with the hint "not too fast, not too slow". A 13 year old on a trackpad would be stuck. Skip Intro rescued me.
- The intro takes a long time before you reach the city.
- "Use a Photo" for a selfie in a game for 13+ worries me.
- The 13+ checkbox is self-declared. Fine, but no real age protection.
- Rosa's tour popup appeared the moment I clicked the street and blocked the screen.

## 11. Scores (out of 10)
Fun 7, New York feel 7, Ease of use 7, Would recommend 4 (for classroom use).
