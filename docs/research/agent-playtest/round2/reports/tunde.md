# Tunde: exit interview

## 1. Arriving
A classmate dropped the link in the program WhatsApp group: "lol is this what they think NYC is". I expected a Club Penguin knockoff with cartoon cringe, probably a US-centric "look how fun New York is" thing. The landing page (Brooklyn Bridge art, "Waddle on over to New York.") looked far more polished than I expected. I declined analytics on the cookie banner.

## 2. What I did
Play Now, then Surprise Me for the avatar (got "Pigeon Grey"), typed my name, ticked the 13+/terms box. Went through the MetroCard photo booth and stamp intro. Rosa the super offered a tour; I said I'd explore alone. Walked around Times Square, opened the map, went to Grand Central (Whispering Gallery, newspaper, stamp book, phone menu), asked chat about Harlem, then jumped to Wall Street and left. About 38 actions.

## 3. Leaving
At Wall Street, action ~38. I had worked out what it is: a well-made, affectionate, walk-and-click-and-collect world. Nothing was pulling me deeper, the chat bots were canned, and my own neighbourhood does not exist in it. I had an assignment to get back to, and I had what I needed to answer the group chat.

## 4. Fun
- Grand Central: the four-faced clock, the constellation ceiling, and the Whispering Gallery actually whispering back. Someone knows the real place.
- The Club NYC Times: "Soup Dumpling Claims Its First Tongue of the Day", "Folded, never forked." Deadpan and correct, not childish.
- Rosa, "your building's super", with "a boiler to fix". Very true.
- Level titles (Day-Tripper, Commuter, next "Bodega Regular") and the daily "MetroCard refill". I like the transit vocabulary.
- The MetroCard photo booth with "No refunds for blinking".

## 5. Static
- Confetti and "stamp earned" bursts for simply walking or arriving. It rewards things that are not achievements.
- NPC chat: I asked about Harlem and the Bronx; GelatoGio said "Best slice in the city. No contest." Obviously a canned bot. I hoped for something that reacted to what I said.
- Dance Jam banner in Times Square: I joined it and got a tutorial, not a dance. I did not find out what it did.
- The map: a pretty picture. Clicking works, but there is nothing to read about places.

## 6. Wanted
- Harlem, Washington Heights, the Bronx, Brooklyn neighbourhoods beyond the bridge; Columbia. The map stops around the Met.
- A way to see real places' stories (history, who lives there), not just tourist landmarks.
- Real people to talk to. Only a few real players (DPriceBK, Lukas_MUC, AngieB) showed up and none spoke.
- A subway map that matches the one I fought with for a month.

## 7. New York
True: Grand Central details, pigeons everywhere, subway entrances, hot dog carts, the super with the boiler, the soup-dumpling joke, folding pizza, the 25 cent paper joke.
False or thin: Manhattan drawn as a squat blob with Liberty Island touching its tip; Central Park a dot; nothing north of the Met; the Charging Bull standing in front of the NYSE (it's at Bowling Green); one block per landmark. It is the visitor's New York. A resident would not spend an afternoon in Times Square.

## 8. Coming back
Maybe, but probably not tomorrow. I would send it to the group chat: "ok it's not what I expected, the Grand Central and newspaper bits are good. Where is Harlem though." I would send it to my classmates who like this kind of thing, not my friends from Lagos.

## 9. One change
Put in real neighbourhoods north of 96th Street, with Harlem first, written with the same care as the Grand Central and newspaper bits.

## 10. Bugs and confusion
- Clicking toolbar icons by guess at the wrong x moved my avatar instead of opening a panel (icons have no labels).
- Dance Jam button left me unsure whether I was dancing or being onboarded.
- The Rosa onboarding card appeared on top of Times Square right after the jam click, which looked like the jam had become a tutorial.

## 11. Scores
- Fun: 6/10
- New York feel: 6/10
- Ease of use: 7/10
- Would recommend: 5/10
