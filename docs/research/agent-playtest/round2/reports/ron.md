# Exit interview: ron (Ron Whitaker, 67, retired history teacher, Tucson)

## 1. Arriving
My grandson emailed the link: "Grandpa you'd like this one". The page showed a big headline, "Waddle on over to New York.", over a painted Central Park. I took it for a cartoon online game about New York. The nostalgia line seemed aimed at my grandson's generation. A cookie box came first and I clicked "Necessary Only". The text was big enough to read comfortably.

## 2. What I did
1. Read the landing page, saw "Free. Coins are earned in the game, never bought", and pressed Play Now.
2. Chose "Surprise Me" instead of the selfie option. I would not send a photo of myself.
3. Accepted the first look, typed a name, and ticked the age/terms box.
4. Did the MetroCard photo booth, stamped the card, and swiped it at the turnstile. My slow mouse failed the first two swipes ("Please swipe again"). The third, faster drag worked.
5. Arrived in Times Square, took Rosa's short tour, clicked a coin, and said hello in chat.
6. Opened the map and went to Grand Central. I read the Opal Clock and Departures cards, and walked into the Whispering Gallery, which echoed and gave me Level 2.
7. Went to the Met through the map. Clicked the pretzel cart (coins went 101 to 99, a speech bubble, nothing else).
8. Opened the newspaper: "The Club NYC Times", dated today, with a soup-dumpling story and a history piece on the 1904 subway opening. Got a Newshound stamp.
9. Left after roughly 45 actions.

## 3. Leaving
I closed the tab on my own. It had been a pleasant half hour, I had read the newspaper, and lunch was ready. Nothing annoyed me. I stopped because I had seen the good part (the facts) and nothing pulled me toward the next thing. I would show my wife later.

## 4. Fun
- The Opal Clock card: real fact (opal faces, "meet me at the clock") plus a small joke. My favorite thing.
- Grand Central itself: zodiac ceiling, brass clock, Whispering Gallery sign, departures board.
- The Whispering Gallery echo ("…hello?") with a level-up.
- The newspaper. Dated today, 25 cents, and the subway story had the right facts (October 27, 1904, a nickel, City Hall to 145th Street). A teacher would use this.
- "No refunds for blinking" in the MetroCard booth, and "Please swipe again at this turnstile".
- The map is clear and pretty, and I could tell where I was.

## 5. Static
- The pretzel cart: coins dropped by 2, a bubble said "Iconic", and nothing else. I wanted to see the pretzel, a short scene, or a fact.
- The Times Square welcome box faded in and out over a crowd of avatars with a dance bubble. It was hard to read. I wanted it on a still background.
- Many toolbar icons have no labels. I had to guess.
- The Dance Jam countdown banner pushed me, and I never knew what it was.

## 6. Wanted
- More time to read the swipe instructions before the card test.
- A way to reread the facts later, such as a collection of the cards I had opened.
- A text-size setting, since my card says large text.
- A reason to keep going after the facts, like a short trail ("Walk the 1904 subway route").

## 7. New York
True, from my 1979 and 2015 trips and from teaching:
- Grand Central's zodiac ceiling, the Opal Clock, the Whispering Gallery, the 44 platforms.
- The Met's steps with hot dog and pretzel carts, autumn leaves, yellow cabs.
- The "please swipe again" turnstile.
- The 1904 subway facts in the newspaper.
- The soup dumpling being hot, and "folded, never forked".

Fake or off:
- It is much cleaner and friendlier than the 1979 city I remember. That is fine for a game.
- Chat showed a stranger asking for a phone number, which the site hid as "[removed]". That was reassuring.

## 8. Coming back
Maybe, leaning yes. I would come back to read more place facts and the next newspaper. Who I would send it to: my old teaching colleagues. The message: "A New York game with real history facts, no cost, no shooting. Try Grand Central and the newspaper."

## 9. One change
Let the first minutes run at my own pace: more tolerance for a slow swipe, and a tour box that does not appear over a crowd of moving characters. After that, give me a trail of facts to follow.

## 10. Bugs and confusion
- The turnstile swipe rejected two slow, careful drags. A slow-handed player may get stuck here.
- The "New in Town 0/5" list still showed 0/5 and "Take a Walk" after I had clicked the ground and earned stamps. I could not tell what counted.
- Welcome overlay text overlapped the scene while fading.
- Test-tool glitch, not a game bug: partway through, the controller briefly returned "unknown agent" and the session was cut off. It was fixed and I carried on from the same spot.

## 11. Scores
- Fun: 8/10
- New York feel: 8/10
- Ease of use: 7/10
- Would recommend: 8/10
