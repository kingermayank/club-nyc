# Marcus Lee: exit interview

## 1. Arriving
An old Astoria friend dropped the link in our group chat: "this made me think of you". I landed on a marketing page with a pixel-art-ish Empire State observation deck over a Central Park skyline, "Waddle on over to New York." and a line about remembering Club Penguin. I figured: Club Penguin reskinned for New York, a nostalgia play. The skyline art looked good. I declined analytics cookies and skipped the scroll. I didn't want to be sold to, I wanted to see if it was any good.

## 2. What I did
1. Clicked Play Now. Skipped "Use a Photo" and "Surprise Me", picked Build My Own (beanie avatar, "Hydrant Red").
2. Named myself CrownHtsMarc, ticked the 13+/terms box, walked in.
3. Intro: photo booth, stamped MetroCard, swiped it (failed once: "Please swipe again at this turnstile"), rode the 1 to Times Square.
4. Rosa (my building's super) offered a tour; I said I'd explore on my own.
5. Times Square: clicked the ground, grabbed a coin, added BigAppleAbby as a friend, opened the map.
6. Map to Chinatown/Little Italy, walked into Joe's Pizza. Ordered a plain slice, then started the Slice Factory shift. Burnt my first pie.
7. Quit the shift, opened the subway map, saw Queens/Brooklyn were only labels, left. About 80 actions, around 14 minutes.

## 3. Leaving
I left at about action 80, on the subway map. The map prints "QUEENS" and "BROOKLYN" in grey letters and you can't go to either. I lived in Astoria and Crown Heights for 11 years. The N to Astoria isn't there, and neither is the A/C or the 2/3/4/5 to Crown Heights. Everything else I'd seen was Midtown and downtown, so it felt like a tourist's New York, not mine. I wasn't angry. I was a bit deflated, which is what I'd have expected from a city I miss. The pizza game was also fiddly enough that I wasn't tempted to grind it for coins.

## 4. Fun
- **The MetroCard swipe.** "Not too fast, not too slow", then "Please swipe again at this turnstile. Every New Yorker hears it on day one. Go again." I laughed. That was the moment I thought the people who made this have lived there. Stamping the card first was a nice tactile beat too.
- **Small NYC details.** A rat in the corner of the platform. The super with "a boiler to fix". NPC lines like "Let's get halal." and "Just saw a rat carry a whole slice. Respect." BigAppleAbby's bio: "Thinks the best bagel is the one in your hand."
- **Joe's Pizza.** The "With a Fork and Knife" option, "fold it like a local", and the "Carb-Jitsu" sandwich board (a Club Penguin wink I enjoyed). The place looked like Little Italy, with Joe's Shanghai and a bubble tea shop on the same corner and a Little Italy arch.
- **Honest labels.** "City local · computer-run" on NPCs. I appreciated not being fooled about who's real.
- **The art overall.** Times Square with the red TKTS stairs, hot dog cart, pigeons and taxi looked right.

## 5. Static
- **Ordering a slice.** I got a speech bubble ("Folded. Dripping. Perfect.") and a stamp. No slice in my hand, no fold, no bite. I wanted to see the thing.
- **"Show Me" from Rosa's checklist.** It highlighted the counter, but clicking where I thought the shop was did nothing (the sandwich board only starts a two-player spot). It took me about four tries to find "Order a Slice". The hover label is the only clue.
- **Level-ups and coins.** Level 2 then 3 within about two minutes, mostly for walking and clicking. It felt like a free confetti machine, so it didn't feel earned.
- **Times Square itself.** Empty and clean. No crowd, no costumed characters, no one trying to hand me a CD. A handful of NPCs and some pigeons.
- **Map.** A nice picture, but a map you click to teleport. No walking between neighborhoods, so no sense of the city as a place.

## 6. Wanted
- To ride the N to Astoria, or any train out of Manhattan. Brooklyn and Queens exist only as words.
- To see and eat the slice, and to pay at a slice counter with the paper plate.
- Bodegas, a bodega cat, a deli sandwich ("chopped cheese"), an egg-and-cheese. Halal cart is mentioned in the tutorial, but I didn't find it.
- A way to pick the pizza game's difficulty or practice with no oops counter. My first pie burnt before I understood the sauce bar.
- A real "yelling at someone to hold the door" interaction. (Not complaining, just a wish.)

## 7. New York
**True:**
- Turnstile swipe failure, in the exact spirit of the real thing.
- Rats, pigeons, the super with a boiler, halal talk.
- Joe's Pizza, Joe's Shanghai, bubble tea, Little Italy arch, bok choy crates.
- Subway tile walls, orange and yellow seats, line strip, "Stand clear of the closing doors, please".
- Rockefeller rink, Grand Central, Brooklyn Bridge, the High Line on the west side.

**False or off:**
- The subway car shows 1 2 3 4 5 6 B D F M A C E on one strip, and I rode the 1 to Times Sq from Times Sq. Nobody rides a car with those lines.
- Times Sq lists 1 2 3 7 N but skips Q R W and S.
- The map is cartoon-squished: Times Sq beside the Empire State and the Statue of Liberty beside Chinatown. Fine for a cartoon, but I'd notice it.
- Real slice shops don't have tablecloth tables and whole pies behind glass. They have slices, paper plates and foldable crust, and nobody sits.
- Manhattan only. Most New Yorkers don't live there, and the version of the city I miss is Queens and Brooklyn.

## 8. Coming back
**Maybe.** I'd come back once if my friend was online, to see Brooklyn or Queens if they appear, and because it's cute and low-pressure. Not daily. Nothing here pulled me back except friends, and a Stardew-style loop would need more than coins and stamps.

I'd send it to the group chat: "ok this is actually funny, the turnstile thing got me. You can't take the N to Astoria yet though."

## 9. One change
Put Queens and Brooklyn on the subway map and make at least one real neighborhood there (Astoria's Ditmars/Broadway, Crown Heights' Franklin Ave, a diner, a bodega) so the map's grey labels turn into places. It would tell me this is about New Yorkers, not Midtown tourists.

## 10. Bugs and confusion
- Rosa's "Show Me" for Work a Shift doesn't make it clear where to click; the shop counter hover label is the only hint.
- Pizza game: I couldn't tell how much to scribble sauce for the bar to count. The belt moves quickly, so one early mistake costs an oops.
- A sandwich board tile that's "clickable" but isn't where you work.
- Subway car lines and Times Sq line list are inconsistent with the real station.
- Intro asks for a photo/selfie option; I skipped it and don't know what happens with it. (Privacy-wise I wanted to know before clicking.)

## 11. Scores (out of 10)
- Fun: 7
- New York feel: 6 (8 for the jokes and details, 4 for the map and Manhattan-only scope)
- Ease of use: 7
- Would recommend: 6
