# Tyler Brooks: exit interview

1. **Arriving**: Found the link on r/WebGames ("made a cozy multiplayer NYC browser game"). The landing page promised a Club Penguin-style nostalgia trip in New York. I figured cozy social hangout, probably light on gameplay. I declined analytics cookies and hit Play Now.

2. **What I did**:
   - Chose Surprise Me, then accepted the default look and typed a name.
   - Ticked the age and terms box.
   - Skipped the MetroCard photo-booth intro.
   - Clicked a coin and got a welcome gift.
   - Tried WASD and the arrow keys. Nothing moved.
   - Clicked the ground to walk.
   - Opened the map and went to Wall Street.
   - Clicked the stock board and the Exchange door. Both were read-only text popups.
   - Opened the phone menu and hit "How to Play", which reopened the same Rosa welcome card.
   - Left.

3. **Leaving**: I left after about 25 actions, around 4 minutes. That is over my two-minute budget. The deciding thing was that I could not find a single mechanic. Movement is click-to-walk only. Every object I clicked gave me a card of text. There was nothing to practice or get better at.

4. **Fun**:
   - The art is very good, and the map is the best screen.
   - The Big Board jokes ("GPUs in stock: zero. Strong buy.") got a smirk.
   - Picking up a coin gave instant feedback (sound, XP, balance going up). That is the only moment that felt like a game.

5. **Static**:
   - MetroCard photo booth intro: a forced mini-scene before I could move. I wanted to be in the city already.
   - Big Board: a popup of prices I cannot trade. I hoped for a real mini stock game.
   - Exchange door: a history card with a "Got It" button. I hoped it would open something to play.
   - "Show Me" and "How to Play": Rosa's card repeated. It did not show me a game.
   - Quest tracker ("Click anywhere on the ground", "Click any New Yorker"): these are tutorials, not goals.

6. **Wanted**:
   - WASD or arrow movement.
   - Something with a skill ceiling, such as a leaderboard, a race or timed scoring.
   - A visible list of the mini-games, so I would not have to hunt for them.
   - To trade the stocks.
   - Anything competitive against other players.

7. **New York**: I have never been there, so I judged it from GTA IV, Spider-Man and NBA broadcasts.
   - Times Square and the Bull and Exchange look the way I expected.
   - Pigeons, hot dog carts, taxis and subway entrances all felt right.
   - The Bull was parked next to a hot dog cart, which I assume is a real thing.
   - It reads like a postcard, not a city. Streets are single rooms I teleport between, so I never got the sense of a big map.

8. **Coming back**: No. Maybe only if someone told me there were real competitive mini-games. I would not send it to my gamer friends. I might send it to my sister with: "cute NYC chat room, you can dress up a little guy, no stress."

9. **One change**: Add WASD movement and put one skill-based mini-game in front of me within the first 60 seconds. Rank it against other players online and show it on the first screen.

10. **Bugs and confusion**:
   - WASD and arrow keys do nothing, and there is no hint that movement is click-only.
   - Right after the MetroCard intro, the Rosa welcome card popped up over the city, so I got two tutorials back to back.
   - "How to Play?" in the phone menu just reopened the Rosa intro instead of explaining controls.
   - Most toolbar icons have no labels. I had to hover to learn they were chat log, Moves and so on.

11. **Scores**: fun 4/10, New York feel 6/10, ease of use 6/10, would recommend 3/10.
