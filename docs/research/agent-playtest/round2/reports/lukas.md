# Lukas Becker (lukas) - exit interview

## 1. Arriving
A link in an urbanism newsletter I subscribe to. I expected a map tool or a city-simulation toy. The landing page said "Waddle on over to New York" and pitched nostalgia for a kids' game. That is not my thing, but the Brooklyn Bridge art was good, and "Coins are earned in the game, never bought" earned some trust. I declined analytics cookies.

## 2. What I did
1. Declined cookies, scrolled the landing page, clicked Play Now as a guest.
2. Built a character (Build My Own, accepted the defaults), named myself, ticked the 13+/terms box.
3. Did the MetroCard intro: photo booth, stamp the card, swipe it (failed the first try, got "Please swipe again"), then rode the 1 train to Times Square.
4. Told Rosa I'd explore alone. Got the "New in Town" stamp.
5. Opened the map, went to Wall Street, and read the Big Board stock panel.
6. Rode the subway to The Met (86 St).
7. Got Level 2 and the Straphanger stamp. Tried to enter the Met by clicking the door and only opened my own player card.
8. Opened the newspaper, then left (about 40 actions in, roughly 7 minutes).

## 3. Leaving
I left after about 40 actions. I had seen the whole loop (walk, stamp, XP, level) and found no system with depth. The Wall Street board was explicitly fake and not tradable. The Met had no interior. The player card opened by accident and Escape didn't close it. It was charming and well written, but not for me, and I had work to do.

## 4. Fun
- The MetroCard intro. "No refunds for blinking" and "Please swipe again at this turnstile" are dry and true, and the drag-to-swipe with a speed check is a good small mechanic.
- The newspaper. It was dated the real date with plausible weather, and the soup-dumpling item was funny. It felt alive.
- The subway map. The line and station logic is surprisingly credible.
- Times Square and The Met art. Handsome, with lots of recognisable detail.

## 5. Static
- The Big Board on Wall Street. The copy is funny, but the prices are "made up". I wanted to buy, sell and see what moves the prices.
- The Met. The big doors do nothing and there is no interior.
- The "20+ Places" and "Dance Jam" calls to action are labels on a page. I didn't try the Dance Jam.
- Most NPC chat lines ("The market's volatile. So is the 4 train.") are one-liners with nothing to respond to.

## 6. Wanted
- A real economy: tradable prices, supply and demand, something where coins have consequences.
- Walking into the Met and the other landmarks.
- Subway detail: transfers, travel time, delays, a real route planner. The map is nice but ride time is just a cutscene.
- A way to see how the city "runs" (population, congestion, schedules) given the urbanism framing of the link.

## 7. New York
True:
- Turnstile "swipe again".
- Pretzel and hot dog carts.
- The Met steps and fountains.
- The Lex 4/6 to Grand Central and Wall St.
- The 42 St shuttle.
- Brooklyn Bridge-City Hall.
- The 4 train jokes.
- The soup dumpling line.

False or stylised:
- Geography is cartoon-compressed: Liberty Island sits just off the Battery and the bridge is at Wall St.
- The map has no Brooklyn or Queens.
- Nitpicks on the subway map: B doesn't serve Wall St, and the 4/6 trunk is simplified.
- Rent is shown as 3,554 coins ("Studio, monthly"), which is oddly cheap given what I remember.

## 8. Coming back
Maybe. I would check back for a market or economy system if the newsletter mentioned one. It isn't for daily play. I might forward it to my colleague who plays Cities: Skylines, as a funny NYC toy: "Cute NYC chat game, great subway intro and the in-game newspaper is genuinely funny. Not deep, but look at the Times Square and Met screens."

## 9. One change
A visible system with consequences I can influence. Make the Wall Street board tradable with a simple model, or let the subway affect something (crowding, travel time, shift income). Also let me walk into the Met.

## 10. Bugs and confusion
- Clicking the Met's door opened my own player card, because I was standing at the door. It looked like nothing happened, and Escape did not close the card. I had to find the small icon.
- Clicking Play Now as a guest went straight into character creation with an age/terms tick-box (fine). The "Use a Photo" option at the start would be a surprise on a desktop with no camera.
- The first swipe attempts failed with little feedback on what speed to use. It worked at medium speed.
- The "Take a Walk" quest completed late, only after clicking the Met door.

## 11. Scores
- Fun: 6/10
- New York feel: 7/10
- Ease of use: 8/10
- Would recommend: 5/10
