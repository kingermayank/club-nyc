# Exit interview: nora (Nora Castellano, 34, licensed NYC guide)

## 1. Arriving
A former tour client emailed the link with "thought of you!". I landed on a cute marketing page: Central Park, Bow Bridge, a Boathouse, pretzel cart, cartoon penguin-style avatars. I thought it was a kids' Club Penguin clone set in NYC. The headline "Waddle on over to New York." made me smile. The cookie banner had a clear "Necessary Only" button, which I took.

## 2. What I did (about 35 actions)
1. Scrolled the landing page: "Explore NYC" place list.
2. Play Now, then "Surprise Me" avatar (got "Pretzel Brown"). Typed a name, ticked the 13+/terms box.
3. MetroCard intro: photo booth, stamped the card, swiped it (failed once: "Please swipe again at this turnstile."), then rode the 1 train to Times Square.
4. Rosa the super offered a tour. I tried it, then skipped it and clicked the ground (Take a Walk +10).
5. Opened the map, went to Grand Central: clicked the clock ("Meet Me at the Clock"), tried the ceiling (nothing), tried the Whispering Gallery.
6. Map to Brooklyn Bridge, clicked the tower for the fact card. Left.

## 3. Leaving
I left after about 6 to 7 minutes, voluntarily, not annoyed. I had checked the three things I would check as a guide (Grand Central, Brooklyn Bridge, Times Square) and the facts held up. Nothing pulled me to keep going: I didn't see a goal that would make me stay (the "New in Town" quest said to go back to Times Square for a hot dog cart, which didn't tempt me). I also had work.

## 4. Fun
- The MetroCard swipe and its "Not too fast, not too slow" and "Please swipe again at this turnstile" line. It was the first time a game made me laugh at something true.
- Grand Central: the zodiac ceiling, four-faced clock, split-flap board. "Meet me at the clock! (Which side? All of them.)" is a joke I've used on tours.
- Brooklyn Bridge fact card: May 24, 1883, 1,595 ft, 21 elephants were all correct, with a good dry tag line.
- "No refunds for blinking" at the photo booth. Rosa as "your building's super" is the right archetype.
- Times Square art: bucket-hatted tourists, pigeons, I Heart NY souvenir shop.

## 5. Static
- Whispering Gallery (Grand Central): I got a "(whispers) hello?" bubble and some coins. I hoped for the real trick: two people at opposite corners hearing each other.
- Grand Central ceiling: not clickable. I hoped for the backward-zodiac story (the painter reversed it, and the MTA says it was a mistake).
- The 1-train ride intro: pretty, but just a picture with a ticker.
- Walking NPC "locals" say one line each; they felt like props.

## 6. Wanted
- A tour/history mode: a short guided walk with the story behind each place (Emily Roebling, the 1904 first subway line, the old Penn Station).
- Staten Island and Queens (my home borough). Staten Island Ferry is missing from what I saw; Liberty ferry is there.
- Ability to talk to the "locals" meaningfully, or ask a question.
- Nothing told me what to do after the tutorial; the quest card pointed me back to Times Square when I was elsewhere.

## 7. New York
True:
- Grand Central's four-faced clock, 1913, "meet me at the clock"; the dining-concourse arches.
- Brooklyn Bridge facts; the pedestrian/bike lane markings; love locks (illegal but real); plank deck.
- Subway: orange/yellow seats, door arrows, line strip, MetroCard turnstile experience, rats on the platform.
- The map layout: Grand Central east of Times Square, High Line on the west side.
- Landing page: Liberty ferry from Battery Park.

Off:
- Whispering Gallery sits on the main level; it's on the lower dining concourse near the Oyster Bar.
- Opal clock "valued 10 to 20 million dollars" is the classic tour claim, but I'd say "reputedly"; the faces have never been publicly appraised.
- "Barnum marched elephants to prove it was safe": the legend, but it followed the E2 stampede panic, which isn't mentioned.
- Joe's Pizza is in Greenwich Village, not Little Italy (landing page lists it next to Chinatown and Little Italy).
- MetroCards are being retired for OMNY; nostalgic, but it's no longer what new arrivals use.
- The 1 train opening gives no starting point; and Times Sq has more lines than shown (Q, R, W, S).
- Manhattan is short and fat on the map (acceptable for a cartoon).

## 8. Coming back
Maybe. I'd come back once to check whether other places (Wall Street, Met, Chinatown) are as accurate; I won't come back daily without a reason to (nothing for me to progress toward, no one I know in there). I would send it to my former client who emailed it, and to a fellow guide: "A cartoon NYC game that gets the details right. Swipe the MetroCard and see how many tries it takes you." I would not send it to a first-time tourist as trip prep.

## 9. One change
Add a real story layer: tapping any landmark opens a 20-second "guide's version" with one lesser-known fact and a link to a real-world source. It would have kept me for an hour; the existing fact cards are already close.

## 10. Bugs and confusion
- The first swipe drag did not move the card far; the card seemed hard to drag until I used the full slot width. The swipe feedback ("All the way through the reader.") helped.
- Sim only: coordinates for the map click went directly to the neighborhood; the "My Place" button was easy to miss.
- Quest card "Pick Up Your Welcome Gift: click Sal's cart in Times Square" persisted while I was in other places with no direction on how to get back.
- Unlabeled toolbar icons; I had to guess the map was the third one.

## 11. Scores
- Fun: 7/10
- New York feel: 8/10
- Ease of use: 7/10
- Would recommend: 7/10
