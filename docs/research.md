# Club NYC: Research Notes

A Club Penguin-style illustrated world set in Manhattan. It is single-player (no chat or multiplayer) and centres on the map, the rooms, the shops inside them and the things you can interact with.

Compiled 2026-10-14. Club Penguin facts come from the Club Penguin Fandom wiki (room pages and the Map page) and general web sources. Filming locations were checked against location guides. See **Sources** at the end.

---

## 0. TL;DR

- Club Penguin (CP) was a set of **single-screen illustrated "rooms"** joined by edge arrows and doors. A **postcard-style island Map** worked as fast travel. Most of the fun was in small clickable details: hidden rooms, pins, mini-games placed inside rooms, catalogs and seasonal parties.
- Manhattan suits this well because **it is also an island.** The CP map becomes a tilted, folded tourist map of Manhattan with the sticky note "Click where you want to go!". The **subway** acts as a second, diegetic fast-travel layer, much as the Mine/Underground sat below CP.
- **Proposed rooms:** 18 Manhattan rooms plus 2 bonus rooms off the island (Coney Island and the Bronx ballpark). Each one maps to a CP room, has 1–3 interiors, a mini-game and a famous film or TV scene.
- **Avatar options:** a **pigeon** is the recommended choice ("Club Pigeon"), since it has the same blobby, recolorable silhouette as a penguin. **Tokens** (old subway tokens) replace coins and **Passport stamps** replace CP stamps.

## 1. How Club Penguin was structured

### 1.1 Timeline (for context)
| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 2005-03-31 | Penguin Chat 3 (precursor) sets the look |
| 2005-10-24 | Club Penguin launches (RocketSnail / New Horizon Interactive) |
| 2007 | Disney acquires CP |
| 2008-11-03 | Map redesign with accurate room icons; Dojo Courtyard added |
| 2010 | Stamps (achievements) introduced |
| 2011-06-27 | Big map redesign adding Places / Games / Shops / Pets filter buttons |
| 2017-03-29 | CP shuts down (succeeded by Club Penguin Island, a Unity game shut down in 2018) |

### 1.2 The Map screen (the canonical design)
- It opened from the **blue folded-map icon** on the toolbar. It also opened automatically if you tried to enter a full room.
- **Visual (2005–2011):** the whole island drawn from an **oblique bird's-eye view**, white snow on a blue sea and framed by a **thick white rounded "postcard" border** with a grey circular **X close button** at top right. A tilted **yellow sticky note** in the bottom-right corner read **"CLICK WHERE YOU WANT TO GO!"** in uppercase comic lettering. See `refs/cp-map-2008.png`.
- Each room appeared as a **miniature illustration of its key building** (lighthouse, boat, Town buildings, Plaza storefronts, snow fort, an igloo for the igloo list). Hovering showed the room's name and clicking teleported you there.
- **Secret rooms:** some icons showed no name on hover. The Iceberg was one. The original Dojo was a tiny clickable spot on the mountain and could only be reached from the map.
- **2011 redesign** (`refs/cp-map-2014.png`): far more geography (mountains, waterfalls, rivers, a pine forest, an igloo village). Filter buttons sat at top left: **Places / Games / Shops / Pets**. Clicking a filter put icons on the map, and after you travelled, a **yellow arrow in the room** pointed at the game, catalog or pet feature.
- **Party notes:** during events a note on the map read "Go There!" and linked to the party's main room. Some events used a full **party map** instead, such as the Fair's Amazement Park or the Future Party.

**Takeaways for room design:**
1. Every room has **2–4 exits** shown as edge arrow signs plus doors, so the island reads as a walkable graph.
2. The **map shortcuts** that graph.
3. Each hub room has 2–3 interiors.
4. **Hidden entrances** (speaker, manhole, boulder, map-only Iceberg) rewarded curiosity.
5. **Mini-games live inside rooms** and start by walking onto a trigger (stepping on the bean bags, sitting in the boat).

### 1.4 Toolbar / HUD (classic)
- **Bottom bar:** Emotes (smiley balloon menu), Actions (wave, dance, sit, throw snowball), chat input + send, **Player card** (your avatar plus inventory or "paper doll" outfit editor), Buddy list, **Igloo** button, **Map** button, Help.
- **Top:** the **Club Penguin Times** newspaper icon (top left), mail/postcards, and the EPF/Spy **phone** (teleports you to HQ rooms).
- **Keyboard shortcuts:** D = dance, W = wave, S = sit, T = throw snowball, plus emote shortcuts.
- **Puffle button** appeared while walking a pet.
- *Club NYC:* keep Map, Apartment (igloo), Emotes, Actions, Wardrobe/Inventory, Newspaper, Stamps book. Drop chat and buddies.

### 1.6 Systems
- **Coins:** earned in mini-games, spent in catalogs. Membership gated most clothing (not relevant for us).
- **Catalogs:** flip-book overlays with page-turn animation. Penguin Style (clothes, Gift Shop), Better Igloos (furniture), Igloo upgrades, Puffle Catalog, Snow & Sports, Music, Costume Trunk, Martial Artworks. **Hidden items** were unlocked by clicking a secret spot on a catalog page, which was a beloved easter egg.
- **Club Penguin Times:** a weekly in-game newspaper with headlines, upcoming events, "Ask Aunt Arctic", jokes, comics and a **Secrets** section with hints to hidden things.
- **Puffles (pets):** round fuzzy pets in colors tied to personalities. You adopted them at the Pet Shop, cared for them (feed, bath, play), walked them, and some unlocked game bonuses.
- **Stamps (2010):** achievements per game and per event, graded Easy / Medium / Hard / Extreme, collected in a Stamp Book shown on your player card.
- **Pins:** a free hidden collectible **placed somewhere different every ~2 weeks**. This is the best cheap mechanic to copy.
- **Mascots:** Rockhopper (pirate visitor), Aunt Arctic (editor), Cadence (DJ), Gary (inventor), Rookie, Herbert (villain polar bear).
- **Parties / seasonal events:** rooms were re-skinned for Halloween, the Holiday Party (Coins for Change charity), Puffle Party, April Fools (Box Dimension), Music Jam, Medieval, Adventure, Fair (Amazement Park), Earth Day, the Waddle On farewell and others. There were temporary rooms, free items and party maps.

## 2. Art style and tech notes

### 2.1 What makes it read as "Club Penguin"
Reference images: `refs/cp-town-center.png`, `refs/cp-coffee-shop.png`, `refs/cp-plaza.png`.

| Trait | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Camera** | A fixed, single-screen, **high-angle 3/4 front view** (not true isometric). The floor fills the lower 2/3 and buildings line the back third, facing the camera. Interiors are viewed from higher, about 45° down. |
| **"Tiny planet" curvature** | Outdoor ground planes **bend down at the left and right edges** like a snow globe. Trees and lamp posts at the edges lean outward slightly, which makes each room feel like a diorama. |
| **Outlines** | **Thick, slightly variable dark outlines**, usually a darker hue of the fill rather than pure black. Interior detail lines are thinner. |
| **Shading** | **Flat cel fills + one shadow tone** (sometimes one highlight). No gradients except sky and lights. |
| **Palette** | Saturated but soft: icy cyan sky, white snow with **lavender/blue-grey shadows**, warm browns for wood and brick, plus pops of magenta, teal and yellow on signage. |
| **Shapes** | Chunky and rounded, slightly squashed or stubby buildings, **wobbly verticals** that lean, oversized doors and windows. |
| **Snow caps** | Thick rounded **snow on every roof, sign, lamp and tree**: a marshmallow layer with a blue shadow underside. *NYC version: use this in winter-party re-skins. Year-round, use water towers, AC units and pigeons on ledges as the "caps".* |
| **Signage** | **Exaggerated, readable hero signs**: vertical "COFFEE", a jukebox-like "DANCECLUB" marquee, "CLOTHES SHOP" in pink. Every building is identifiable from its sign. A heavy rounded sans was used (CP's UI font was Burbank Small Bold). |
| **Exits** | Small **blue arrow signposts** at screen edges; a green "EXIT" sign over interior doors. |
| **Props** | Every room is crowded with clickable props (couches, crates, fruit, posters) that make it feel lived in. Props near the camera act as **foreground occluders**. |

### 2.2 Avatars and movement
- **Click-to-walk:** you click a spot and the avatar walks a straight line, or pathfinds around blocked areas, to the nearest walkable point. Walking off-screen through an arrow zone loads the adjacent room.
- **No perspective scaling:** penguins stay the **same size** anywhere in the room. Depth comes only from **y-sorting** (lower on screen draws in front) and foreground occluder layers. This keeps sprites cheap and readable, and Club NYC should copy it.
- Sprites have **8 directions** with walk, idle, sit, wave and dance cycles. Clothing is **layered paper-doll sprites** (head, face, neck, body, hand, feet slots) drawn over a recolorable body.
- Short **speech/emote balloons** appear over the head.

### 2.3 How it was built
- **Flash (SWF):** the client was Flash. The shell loaded **each room as its own SWF** alongside separate SWFs for the interface, inventory and so on. Community/CPPS sources describe a legacy **ActionScript 2** client and a later **ActionScript 3** client (around 2012). The exact switch date is not well documented.
- **Stage:** a fixed **760×480** stage, commonly cited and consistent with screenshots. Rooms are one screen and never scroll.
- **Room composition:** layered vector art (background, mid props, foreground occluders) plus:
  - a **walkable "block" mask** (polygon or bitmap) that limits where avatars can stand,
  - **trigger hit areas** (doors, edge exits, mini-game seats, hidden spots) that fire on click or on walk-over,
  - **frame-scripted animations** for ambient life and party states (for example, the Night Club "party mode" when the room is crowded).
- **Server:** SmartFoxServer (Penguin Chat used ElectroServer). Rooms had population caps, and a full room redirected you to the map. Not needed for Club NYC.

**Modern equivalent for Club NYC (suggestion):**
- HTML5 canvas or WebGL (PixiJS or Phaser), with a fixed 16:10 stage letterboxed to the window.
- Each room is a JSON file holding: layer images (PNG/SVG), a walkable polygon / navmesh, an exits list (`{zone, targetRoom, spawnPoint}`), hotspots (`{shape, action}`) and a y-sort depth line for each prop.
- A* pathfinding on the navmesh.

## 3. Manhattan room plan

### 3.1 Design rules
- **18 Manhattan rooms + 2 bonus off-island rooms.** That is roughly CP's outdoor-room count, with interiors on top.
- **Parody names, not trademarks:** "Halal Bros" (not Halal Guys), "Robin's Egg & Co." (not Tiffany), "Daily Bagel" (not the Daily Bugle), "Shake Shed", "Toy Palace", "The Piazza Hotel", "Dollar Slice Palace".
- **Film/TV scenes** are listed as verified real filming locations, for easter-egg inspiration. Make the nods **visual homages** (a pose, a prop, a sign) and do not use actual characters or footage.
- The **9/11 Memorial is treated respectfully:** no mini-game, no jokes, ambient and quiet.

### 3.3 Room details

#### 1. Times Square: the spawn hub (Town Center)
- **Interiors:** **Broadway Theater** (CP Stage: rotating mini-plays and a costume trunk); **Candy Tower** (multi-floor candy store, Bean Counters-style catch game); **TIX booth** under the red glass steps (sells "show tickets" that unlock theater costumes).
- **Street vendors:** **Halal Bros** cart (white-sauce gag, line of pigeons); pretzel and hot dog cart.
- **Interactive:** sit on the **red steps**; click billboards to cycle ads (one shows *your* avatar); a generic **"Undies Cowpoke"** guitar busker; costumed-character crowd ("Fuzzy Red Monster", a knock-off superhero) who ask for tips; yellow cabs that honk when clicked; a manhole with steam.
- **Mini-game:** **"Billboard Blitz"**, a light-up sign puzzle (match colors to relight a broken billboard), or **"Cab Dodger"** crossing 7th Ave.
- **Seasonal:** **New Year's Eve Ball Drop** party with a countdown and confetti.
- **Screen moments:** *Taxi Driver* (1976, Travis cruising 42nd St); *Vanilla Sky* (2001, Tom Cruise in an emptied Times Square); *Enchanted* (2007, Giselle climbs out of a Times Square manhole). Easter egg: click the manhole and a princess-dressed pigeon pops out.

#### 2. Rockefeller Center: the ice rink (Ice Rink + Stage + Beacon)
- **Interiors:** **Top of the Rock** observation deck (a Beacon-style sub-room with coin binoculars that frame Central Park and the Empire State); **TV Studio 8-ish** for a live sketch-show set (the audience-applause sign is clickable); **Toy Palace** with a **giant floor piano** (the *Big* homage: walk on keys to play "Heart and Soul"/"Chopsticks").
- **Interactive:** skate on the rink (switches to skating movement); golden **"Titan" statue** (Prometheus homage) above the rink; flags around the plaza; the Atlas-style statue across Fifth.
- **Mini-game:** **"Rink Rush"**, skating laps while collecting tokens and dodging wobbly tourists. There is also a Zamboni-style resurfacing puzzle.
- **Seasonal:** the **Tree Lighting** party (holiday re-skin, snow caps everywhere).
- **Screen moments:** *Elf* (2003, Buddy skates the rink); *Home Alone 2* (1992, Kevin at the giant tree); *The Devil Wears Prada* (2006, Runway's "Elias-Clark" HQ exterior was **1221 Avenue of the Americas**, part of the Rockefeller Center complex); *30 Rock* and SNL (Studio 8H). *Big* (1988): the floor-piano scene was shot at FAO Schwarz's former **767 Fifth Ave** store. The store has since moved to **30 Rockefeller Plaza**, so placing it here is accurate today.

#### 3. Fifth Avenue: the shopping strip (Gift Shop / Mall, the main clothing catalog)
- **Interiors:** **Robin's Egg & Co.** jeweler (robin's-egg blue; easter egg: eat a croissant at the window for a "breakfast" stamp); **Grand Department Store** (main **clothing catalog**, monthly rotation); **The Piazza Hotel** lobby (the Home Alone 2 homage: a concierge who suspects you, a hotel-room service cart).
- **Interactive:** famous **holiday windows** (seasonal animated vignettes you click through); cathedral steps (St. Patrick's homage); horse-drawn carriages at the park corner.
- **Mini-game:** **"Window Dresser"**, a dress-the-mannequin-to-order game (like Pizzatron, but for outfits).
- **Seasonal:** the **Easter Parade** with bonnet contest (a real Fifth Ave tradition); holiday windows.
- **Screen moments:** *Breakfast at Tiffany's* (1961, Holly at the Fifth Ave window); *Home Alone 2* (Plaza Hotel at Fifth & 59th); *Big* (FAO at 767 Fifth).

#### 4. Library & Bryant Park (Coffee Shop + Book Room)
- **Interiors:** **The Great Reading Room** (Book Room: readable in-game books such as NYC trivia and the newspaper archive, chess tables in place of Mancala); **Bryant Park café kiosk** (Bean Counters becomes **"Bagel Counters"**: catch bagels and stack them on the delivery bike, dodge pigeons).
- **Interactive:** two **stone lions** at the steps (click to name them; the real ones are Patience and Fortitude); park carousel; movable green chairs; winter village rink and holiday market (seasonal).
- **Easter egg:** a floating ghost librarian in the stacks shushes you, then flings cards (Ghostbusters homage, no IP).
- **Screen moments:** *Ghostbusters* (1984, opening library haunting at the NYPL main branch); *Breakfast at Tiffany's* (Holly and Paul visit the library); *The Day After Tomorrow* (2004, survivors burn books in the library); *Sex and the City: The Movie* (2008, the jilted library wedding).

#### 5. Grand Central Terminal (Plaza hub + subway/map hub)
- **Interiors:** **Main Concourse** (the four-faced opal **clock**; the **backwards constellation ceiling** — real: the zodiac mural is painted mirror-reversed, which is a great "notice it" stamp); **Whispering Gallery** (stand in one corner and your whisper is heard in the opposite corner, a real acoustic quirk); **Oyster Bar** (vaulted tile); **Summit-style observatory** next door (mirror rooms, glass sky box, a second "Beacon").
- **Interactive:** departure board (click to "check trains", which opens the subway map); the information-booth clock; commuters rushing.
- **Mini-game:** **"Rush Hour"**, sorting commuters onto the right track before departure (a Puffle Roundup-style herding game).
- **Screen moments:** *North by Northwest* (1959, Thornhill flees through the terminal); *The Fisher King* (1991, the commuter waltz); *Gossip Girl* (2007 pilot, Serena returns via Grand Central).

#### 6. Empire State Building (Lighthouse → Beacon)
- **Interiors:** **Art-deco lobby** (gold ceiling mural, model of the building); **86th Floor Deck** (open-air Beacon with coin binoculars, wind blows your hat off); **102nd Floor** (secret, reached from a staff elevator easter egg).
- **Interactive:** **Tower Lights panel**: choose the spire's color scheme (the real tower changes colors for holidays and events) and it stays that color on the map.
- **Mini-game:** **"Big Ape Climb"** (vertical climber dodging biplanes, a King Kong homage), or **"Skyline Jetpack"** (Jet Pack Adventure clone flying over Midtown roofs and water towers).
- **Screen moments:** *King Kong* (1933); *Sleepless in Seattle* (1993, observation-deck rendezvous); *Elf* (Buddy's dad's office is in the Empire State); *An Affair to Remember* (1957).

#### 7. Central Park: The Pond & Wollman Rink (Ski Village + Ski Hill)
- **Interiors / sub-areas:** **Wollman Rink** (winter) / summer funfair; **Pilgrim Hill** (Ski Hill: Central Park's real sledding hill); **Central Park Zoo** gate (sea-lion pool cameo, with penguins in a nod to Club Penguin).
- **Interactive:** **Gapstow Bridge** (photo spot); horse carriages; squirrels that steal your snack; snowball fights (winter); a **pigeon lady** on a bench feeding birds (Home Alone 2 homage).
- **Mini-game:** **"Pilgrim Hill Sled Race"** (a direct Sled Racing analog).
- **Screen moments:** *Home Alone 2* (Kevin with the Pigeon Lady; Gapstow Bridge); *Elf* (sleigh crash in Central Park); *When Harry Met Sally* (1989, autumn-leaves walk).

#### 8. Central Park: Bethesda Terrace & Boathouse (Ski Lodge / Cove / Forest)
- **Interiors:** **The Boathouse** (Ski Lodge: lakeside café with fireplace, rowboat rental, Find Four tables, a cuckoo-clock-style clock with a pigeon that pops out); **Belvedere Castle** (Lodge Attic / lookout tower with weather instruments; the real castle is a weather station).
- **Interactive:** **Bethesda Fountain** (toss a token and make a wish for a random emote); a **Bow Bridge** proposal spot; buskers in the arcade tunnel (Minton tile ceiling); turtles in the lake.
- **Mini-game:** **"Rowboat Fishing"** (an Ice Fishing analog: catch fish and junk, avoid the ducks), or **"Row Row Rumble"** bumper boats.
- **Screen moments:** *Enchanted* ("That's How You Know" at Bethesda Terrace); *Sex and the City* (Carrie and Big tumble into the lake at the Loeb Boathouse).

#### 9. Museum Mile: the Met steps (Dojo + Book Room)
- **Interiors:** **Egyptian Wing / Temple Hall** (a Temple of Dendur homage); **Arms & Armor hall** (knights on horseback); **Rooftop garden** (skyline view). The **Natural History museum** across the park could be a bonus door with a dinosaur skeleton that blinks when you look away.
- **Interactive:** the grand **front steps** (sit and eat a hot dog from the cart; a "Gossip Girl"-style lunch-on-the-steps stamp); fountains; a sketching student who draws your avatar.
- **Mini-game:** **"Art-Jitsu"**, a Card-Jitsu-style card battle with famous-art-style cards (Sculpture > Painting > Photography > Sculpture); belts become "curator badges".
- **Screen moments:** *When Harry Met Sally* (Temple of Dendur scene at the Met); *Ocean's 8* (2018, Met Gala heist, shot inside the Met); *Gossip Girl* (the Met steps); *Night at the Museum* (2006, exterior was the American Museum of Natural History; interiors were built on soundstages).

#### 10. Flatiron & Madison Square Park (Snow Forts crossroads + newspaper HQ)
- **Interiors:** **The Daily Bagel** newspaper office inside the Flatiron (the **in-game newspaper HQ**, a Club Penguin Times / Book Room analog with a printing press and editor desk; homage to the Raimi Spider-Man "Daily Bugle"); **Shake Shed** burger kiosk in the park (the real Shake Shack began as a hot-dog cart in Madison Square Park).
- **Interactive:** the **"wind tunnel"** corner (the Flatiron's 23rd St corner is famously gusty, so your hat blows away); a park dog run; Madison Square Park art installation (rotates seasonally).
- **Mini-game:** **"Shake Shed Rush"** (assemble burger orders) or **"Snowball Forts"** (winter: snowball fight against pigeons from two benches, with a clock-tower target on the Met Life tower).
- **Screen moments:** *Spider-Man* (2002–2007 Raimi films used the Flatiron, digitally altered, as the Daily Bugle building).

#### 11. High Line & Chelsea Market (Forest + Coffee Shop)
- **Interiors:** **Chelsea Market food hall** (the old Nabisco factory, where the Oreo was invented: brick tunnel, waterfall pipe, tacos, lobster, donuts); a TV kitchen studio upstairs (Food Network homage).
- **Interactive:** walk the **elevated rail tracks** through wildflowers; wooden sun loungers that roll on the rails; art installations; the "10th Ave Square" window seats overlooking traffic (cabs you can wave at). The **Hudson River piers / Little Island** sit at the edge.
- **Mini-game:** **"Taco Counters"** (stacking, a Bean Counters analog) or **"Rail Garden"** (water plants along the tracks before a heat-wave timer runs out).
- **Screen moments:** *Set It Up* (2018, Harper and Charlie plot on the High Line); *Broad City* (S2 "Coat Check"); *Side Effects* (2013, brief).

#### 12. Greenwich Village & Washington Square Park (Night Club + Stage)
- **Interiors:** **The Blue Notion** basement jazz club (Night Club: the dance floor lights up and the "party mode" crowd triggers a jam session); **Comedy Basement** (open-mic, with a joke book as a stamp collection); **Petunia Bakery** cupcakes (Magnolia homage); **"the Friends building"** on a corner (a click-only exterior homage, with a coffeehouse with an orange couch next door).
- **Interactive:** the **Arch** and fountain (summer: kids in the spray); **chess hustlers** (Find Four becomes speed chess); a **piano busker** who wheels a grand piano into the park; NYU violet flags; brownstone stoops (sit on Carrie's stoop: the SATC homage on Perry St).
- **Mini-game:** **"Jazz Jam"** (Dance Contest-style rhythm game with sax, upright bass and drums).
- **Seasonal:** the **Village Halloween Parade** (a real 6th Ave parade): Halloween party room.
- **Screen moments:** *When Harry Met Sally* (Harry and Sally part at the Washington Square Arch); *I Am Legend* (2007, Neville's home on Washington Square North); *Friends* (exterior at Bedford & Grove); *Sex and the City* (Carrie's stoop on Perry St; the Magnolia Bakery cupcake scene).

#### 13. SoHo (Clothes Shop / Mall)
- **Interiors:** **Sneaker Vault** (shoes and streetwear catalog, with limited "drops" each in-game week); **Gallery Loft** (art you can buy as furniture); **Pottery Loft** (wheel studio).
- **Interactive:** **cast-iron facades** (knock and they ring hollow); cobblestones; street artists and Prince St jewelry tables; fashion-week photographers who snap you if your outfit matches the "trend of the day".
- **Mini-game:** **"Pottery Wheel"** (a timing/shape game; the *Ghost* homage) or **"Street Style Snap"** (pose-matching).
- **Screen moments:** *Ghost* (1990, Sam and Molly's loft at **102 Prince St**, SoHo).

#### 14. Chinatown & Little Italy (+ Lower East Side deli) (Pizza Parlor + Pet Shop)
- **Interiors:** **Dollar Slice Palace** (Pizza Parlor: **"Slice-o-Tron 3000"**, a Pizzatron clone with a secret **cannoli mode**); **Golden Dumpling House** (fold-dumpling mini-game); **Klatz's Deli** (LES pastrami deli with a hanging sign over one table, the *When Harry Met Sally* "I'll have what she's having" homage; sit there to get a stamp); **Pet Bodega** (adopt **bodega cats**: the Puffle analog).
- **Interactive:** **Mott St** fruit stands; Canal St knock-off stalls (fake "Pucci" bags gag); Columbus Park mahjong and erhu players; fortune cookies (random fortunes); Mulberry St red-white-green arches.
- **Seasonal:** **Lunar New Year** (lion dance, firecracker confetti) and the **Feast of San Gennaro** (September street fair: zeppole, games of skill).
- **Screen moments:** *The Godfather* (1972, Vito Corleone is shot at a fruit stand on **Mott St**: the stand at 137 Mott, Genco office at 128 Mott); *When Harry Met Sally* (Katz's Deli, Houston St).

#### 15. Brooklyn Bridge (→ DUMBO) (Dock-like transit room)
- **Sub-room:** **DUMBO** (off-island): the cobblestone Washington St view framing the Manhattan Bridge (a photo spot), a waterfront carousel and pebble beach with a skyline view.
- **Interactive:** the elevated wooden promenade; a **bike lane** gag (step in it and a bell dings, a cyclist yells); vendors selling "I-heart-NYC"-style shirts (use a generic heart); padlocks (a real tradition the city removes, so they vanish each week).
- **Mini-game:** **"Bike Lane Dash"** (an endless runner dodging cyclists and selfie sticks).
- **Screen moments:** *Kate & Leopold* (2001, Leopold falls from the Brooklyn Bridge); *Sex and the City: The Movie* (2008, Miranda and Steve reconcile mid-bridge); *Once Upon a Time in America* (1984, the famous Washington St / Manhattan Bridge shot in DUMBO).

#### 16. One World Trade Center & 9/11 Memorial (contemplative + observatory)
- **Interiors:** **Sky observatory** (100+ floors; the elevator ride animates the skyline's history as a timelapse, a nice nod to the real one); **The Oculus** (white-ribbed hall: a quiet shopping concourse plus PATH and subway connections).
- **Memorial pools:** ambient water sound and names on the bronze parapets. The only interaction is **placing a white rose** (a real tradition on victims' birthdays). **No mini-game, no comedy, no pigeons doing bits here.**
- **Screen moment:** *The Walk* (2015, dramatization of Philippe Petit's 1974 high-wire walk; it is about the original towers). Keep this to an optional plaque, or omit it.

#### 17. Battery Park & Ferry Terminal (Dock + Beach)
- **Interiors:** **Castle Clinton** (round sandstone fort, ticket booth for the Statue ferry); **Staten Island Ferry terminal** (big orange boat: ride it for a skyline cutscene).
- **Interactive:** coin-op telescopes facing the Statue; the **bronze bull** near Bowling Green (rub its nose for luck, which grants a random emote); seagulls stealing fries; street performers (the gold-painted "living statue" busker).
- **Mini-game:** **"Ferry Hopper"** (Hydro Hopper: tow-tube behind a water taxi, dodging buoys and tugboats).
- **Screen moments:** *Working Girl* (1988, opens with Tess on the Staten Island Ferry); *Ghostbusters II* (1989, the Statue of Liberty walks ashore at Battery Park).

#### 18. Subway Underground (Mine + Underground + Boiler Room)
- **Interiors:** a **station platform** (the fast-travel UI, see §4); **express tunnel**; the abandoned **City Hall Loop station** (secret: real, closed in 1945, with Guastavino vaulted tiles and skylights; riders could glimpse it by staying on the 6 train as it loops); **Track maintenance room** (Boiler Room analog: an old newspaper archive of past in-game events).
- **Interactive:** a "please swipe again" turnstile gag; "Stand clear of the closing doors" chime; buskers on the mezzanine; **"Showtime!" dancers** in the car (pole-flip emote unlock); rats on the tracks (one carries a pizza slice).
- **Mini-game:** **"Express Track"** (Cart Surfer-style hand-car rail runner with tricks; do not name it "Subway Surfers", which is a trademark) and **"Lost Cat Rescue"** (a Puffle Rescue analog: guide a lost bodega cat back to the platform).
- **Screen moments:** *The Taking of Pelham One Two Three* (1974); *Ghost* (1990, subway poltergeist); *The Warriors* (1979, gang flees through the subway).

#### B1. Liberty Island: the secret room (Iceberg)
- **Not reachable on foot**, only via the ferry or a nameless hover icon on the map.
- **Myth mechanic (a "tip the iceberg" analog):** rumors in the newspaper say that if you stand on the torch it opens. The real torch has been **closed to visitors since 1916**. Make it impossible for most of the game, then make it true during the finale party.
- **Mini-game:** **"Harbor Grabber"** (an Aqua Grabber clone: dive for lost tourist hats, coins and old ferry tokens).
- **Screen moments:** *X-Men* (2000, climax at the Statue); *Saboteur* (1942, Hitchcock).

#### B2. Coney Island (off-island bonus)
- Boardwalk, a big Ferris wheel and an old wooden coaster (generic names), freak-show banners, the beach.
- **Mini-game:** **"Hot Dog Showdown"** (a July 4th eating-contest button masher, a nod to Nathan's) or coaster rail-rider.
- **Seasonal:** the Mermaid Parade (June).
- **Screen moments:** *The Warriors* (1979, the gang's home turf); *Annie Hall* (1977, Alvy's childhood home under the coaster).

#### B3. Bronx Ballpark (off-island bonus)
- A Stadium analog: the bleachers "roll call" chant easter egg, a hot dog vendor throw game, home-run derby mini-game.
- **Screen moments:** *The Pride of the Yankees* (1942, original stadium); *Seinfeld* (George's Yankees front-office job).

## 4. Transit and navigation

### 4.1 Three layers (mirrors CP's map + walking + phone)
1. **Walk** (the default): edge arrows between neighbouring rooms, shown as **street-sign arrows** (green NYC street-name blades, e.g. "← W 42 St").
2. **Tourist Map** (instant, like the CP Map): a **folded paper map of Manhattan** seen from an oblique bird's-eye view. Manhattan's street grid is rotated about **29° from true north**, so tilting the island diagonally, as CP's island was framed, looks natural. Show Battery and the Statue at the bottom left, Harlem fading into clouds at the top, the Hudson and NJ to the left, the East River and Brooklyn and Queens to the right. Each room has a miniature landmark icon.
   - Frame it with a **postcard border**, large "Greetings from Club NYC" letters and a tilted **yellow sticky note: "Click where you want to go!"** (direct homage), or a variant like "Where to, buddy?" written on a cab receipt.
   - Liberty Island is the **nameless secret icon**.
   - Filter tabs: **Places / Games / Shops / Pets** (copy the 2011 CP design).
3. **Subway** (the diegetic fast travel): enter any station stair to reach the **Platform room**. Pick a stop on a **stylized subway diagram**, then play a short train-car interlude (random easter eggs: Showtime dancers, mariachi trio, a man with a full drum kit, "empty car" smell gag), then arrive at the station exit in the target room.

### 4.2 Extras
- **Yellow cab:** hail from any street room (raise-arm emote; a cab is "available" only when its **roof light is lit**, which is real NYC etiquette). It drives to any room with a meter-ticking animation and is free. It is a joke version of the map.
- **Ferries:** Battery → Liberty Island (secret); **Staten Island Ferry** (free real-world ride, here a skyline cutscene that returns to Battery); **NYC-style water taxi** to DUMBO and Coney Island.
- **Roosevelt Island aerial tram:** a bonus ride with a view of the Queensboro Bridge (the Spider-Man 2002 climax used the tram and the Queensboro Bridge).
- **Bike share:** speed-walk power-up in park rooms.

### 4.3 Subway diagram (stylized, approximate)
Use a **Vignelli-1972-style diagram**: 45/90° lines, colored circles, white background. Draw custom bullets rather than copying official MTA artwork.

| Line color | Lines | Club NYC rooms served (approximate) |
|---|---|---|
| Red | 1 / 2 / 3 | Battery (South Ferry), WTC (Cortlandt), Village (Christopher St), Times Sq, Columbus Circle (Central Park) |
| Green | 4 / 5 / 6 | Bowling Green (Battery), Brooklyn Bridge–City Hall (+ secret City Hall Loop), Canal St (6, Chinatown), 23 St (6, Flatiron), Grand Central, 86 St (Met), Bronx Ballpark (4) |

## 5. NYC easter eggs and delight

### 5.1 Ambient life (every street room)
- **Pigeons** that scatter when you walk through, then re-land. If you play as a pigeon, they flock behind you.
- **Steam from manholes** and orange-and-white steam stacks (Con Ed's real district steam system). Clicking one gives a "sauna" emote.
- **Yellow cabs** honking; **"Don't Honk — $350 penalty"** signs (real NYC signage) that get honked at anyway.
- **Hot dog carts** ("dirty water dogs"), **halal carts**, pretzel carts, an ice-cream truck with a looping jingle ("Mister Frosty", a parody).
- **Rats**, rarely carrying a whole pizza slice down stairs: the **Pizza Rat** cameo (2015 viral video from a Manhattan subway stair). Make it a rare sighting stamp.
- **Bodega cats** sleeping on chip shelves; the bodega guy asks "the usual?" and serves a **bacon-egg-and-cheese**.
- **Water towers** on roofs; **fire escapes** with laundry and plants; **brownstone stoops** you can sit on.
- **Open fire hydrant** spray in summer rooms (kids run through it).
- **Delivery e-bikes** zipping past; **trash-bag mountains** on the curb (a jump-on easter egg).
- **Walk / Don't Walk** signals: jaywalk at your own risk and a cab beeps. True New Yorkers cross on the flashing hand.
- **Squirrels** in park rooms that steal snacks.

### 5.2 Characters and performers (generic, no real people)
- **"Undies Cowpoke":** guitar busker in briefs and a cowboy hat (Times Square).
- **"Showtime!" crew:** acrobatic dancers on the subway pole ("What time is it? SHOWTIME!"), unlocking a pole-flip emote.
- **Mariachi trio** in subway cars; **bucket drummers** on the mezzanine.
- **Chess hustlers** and the **piano busker** in Washington Square.
- **Costumed characters** in Times Square (knock-off fuzzy red monster, a mouse, a superhero) who demand tips for photos.
- **Living-statue** gold busker at Battery Park.
- **Tour-bus guide** with a megaphone on an open-top double-decker (tours the map like CP's Tour Booth guides).
- **Street preacher**, **sample-sale line**, a **dog walker** with 10 leashes tangled around you.

### 5.3 Secret / "notice it" stamps
- Grand Central's **ceiling zodiac is reversed**.
- **Whispering Gallery** corner-to-corner whisper.
- Ride the 6 train past the last stop to see the **City Hall Loop station**.
- The **Statue's torch** has been closed since 1916 (the tip-the-iceberg-style myth).
- **Empire State tower lights**: set them to your favorite color.
- The **Flatiron corner wind** steals your hat.
- **Bethesda Fountain** wish.
- Sit at the "what she's having" table in the deli.
- Rub the bronze bull's nose.
- Stand on the **floor piano** and play the melody.
- Find all 5 hidden **MetroCards** in a week.
- Order a **chopped cheese** at a bodega (inside joke).
- Swipe your card and get "Please swipe again" three times in a row.

### 5.4 Playable avatar concepts
| Option | Pitch | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Club Pigeon** (recommended) | Every player is a NYC pigeon | Same blobby, two-feet, recolorable silhouette as a CP penguin; rhymes with "penguin"; head-bob walk cycle and coo emotes are inherently funny; pigeons are everywhere in NYC so it fits every room | Must make pigeons cute: round body, stubby legs, iridescent neck ring as the "color" |
| Club Rat | Play as a rat (Pizza Rat energy) | Iconic, mischievous | Gross for some, smaller appeal |
| Bodega Cat | Play as a cat | Cute, NYC-specific | Then pets need a new concept |
| Bagel / Food People | Blob-shaped characters (bagel, pretzel, slice) | Silly, merchandisable | Less "creature", outfits harder to fit |
| Generic "New Yorker" blob | Simple bean-shaped person | Most flexible for clothing | Loses the CP animal charm |

**Pigeon avatar notes:** the body color is recolorable like CP penguins (classic grey, white "rock dove", brown "checker", pink, teal…); clothing uses the same paper-doll slots; emotes include coo, head bob, puff chest, peck a crumb; the "throw snowball" action becomes **throw breadcrumbs** (other pigeon NPCs swarm the landing spot).
