### The Exchange and the Opening Bell {#opening-bell}

Click the Exchange to walk inside, onto the trading floor of the New York Stock Exchange: trading pods ringed with live charts, screens hanging from the ceiling and a price crawl under the big screen. Every day a real company that recently went public gets the bell, from the public IPO calendar, and it's the same company for everyone. Its ticker and offer price are on the big screen over the podium; the outdoor ticker shows it too, as **Today's IPO**. Prices are real but delayed about 15 minutes, and the tape under the screen carries New York names like Shake Shack, the New York Times and Ralph Lauren, plus the next big IPO on the calendar. If the market feed is down, the floor lists one of the city's own invented companies instead (Bodega Cat Holdings, anyone?).

To ring the bell, click the podium. You walk up the stairs, and the screen counts down from 9:29:55. When it reads **9:30:00**, press the red button and hold it (or hold Space). The bell rings as long as you hold, up to about ten seconds, like the real one. When you let go, the floor cheers and the new stock starts trading on the screen. Press too early and the floor will let you know.

The balcony tickers show the month's IPO class and how each one has done since it priced. The trading posts on either side open the Big Board.

### The Big Board {#big-board}

The big board tracks what the startup world won't stop talking about: the AI labs, the IPO class of 2025 and 2026 (SpaceX, Figma, CoreWeave, Klarna) and, of course, the rent. Prices are in Club NYC coins and made up, so it's a joke, not financial advice. Click the board to see it up close: ten stocks ticking live. To play **Call It**, pick a stock, then call it **Up** or **Down** (or press the arrow keys). Six seconds later the board settles it. Being right pays nothing but bragging rights, which on Wall Street is the point; call it right five times in a row for the **Called It** stamp. GPUs are sold out, so there's nothing to call, and calling the rent doesn't count: it only goes up.

### The Bull {#the-bull}

In 1989 an artist dropped a 7,000-pound bronze bull near the stock exchange overnight, as a surprise for the city. Rubbing a bull for luck is a New York tradition, and so is the line to do it. Rub it once for the **Bull Market** stamp. The view zooms in on his face; rub back and forth across the glowing ring on his nose until it fills with gold.
